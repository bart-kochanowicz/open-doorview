## Battery and power frames (2026-11-20 to 2026-11-22)

- The battery capacity scale. The coprocessor log suggests a percentage computed from the battery voltage (raw 458 empty, 475 low, 558 full; see [the factory firmware](factory-firmware.md#battery-scale-from-the-coprocessor-log)). On 2026-11-22 the value fell from 100 to 51 in 8 h 50 min on battery, read 13 for one minute with the screen on and 88 on the charger; so a reading under load or while charging is not the charge state. After the last reboot (2026-11-20) the MCU reported `status=1, usb=0, capacity=100` (on battery) in every PowerInfo frame for at least 3 minutes while the camera was connected to USB (confirmed by the owner); in earlier boots the frames showed `status=2, usb=1`. The cause is unknown, so Home Assistant's External power can be wrong for a while after a boot.

In the first 1.2 hours without anyone at the door the camera did not wake once; during the tests of that day it woke 37 times in 6 hours, most of them on purpose or with the sleep blocked for service.

## Early-boot resets after a software reboot (2026-11-20, 2026-11-23)

- The cause of the early-boot resets. On 2026-11-20 **every one of three software reboots** (`reboot`) showed two resets before the third attempt reached the login prompt (three "System startup" banners, one "Welcome to OpenIPC" per capture; the resets come after "Starting mdev..." or earlier and before any Doorview hook). Whether a cold start (power removed) behaves the same was not tested. The camera was on a USB supply (ISDT Power 200H) throughout. **Observed again on 2026-11-23 with a timestamped UART capture** (one software reboot, on USB power): the first boot reset itself 3 s after "Starting mdev..." (about 10 s after the kernel started); the second reached U-Boot, which printed `Bootlimit (2) exceeded. Using altbootcmd.` and `Boot limit exceeded -- entering FAILSAFE (network + SSH only)`, and then sat there for 71 s until the coprocessor reset the SoC (the "mcu check soc alive" guard, assumed from the 70 s of the factory log); the third boot reached the login prompt. So the second reset is U-Boot's boot counter, not a fault of its own; what makes the first boot reset is still unknown (it falls in the Doorview module phase). The software reboot cost 85 s this way.

## Boot time, first measurements (2026-11-23)

- Boot time of a clean boot, measured on 2026-11-23 from two timestamped UART captures of software reboots, with the broker's log (UTC, whole seconds, the host clock set the camera's time of reboot) for the last step. Seconds after the final U-Boot banner: kernel starts at 3.6 (a 1 s autoboot delay, a USB scan and a flash scan; one capture also showed the boot-limit notice), the root file system is mounted at 6.6 (the ethernet MAC driver waits 0.9 s for a PHY that is not there), the Wi-Fi station request is queued at 9.9, Majestic starts at 11.4 and **the camera connects to the broker 0.25 s after that, 11.6 s after the reset**; the Wi-Fi association and DHCP took about 1.7 s. The user-space scripts from the root mount to Majestic take about 4.7 s (seeding 0.9, fake-hwclock 0.5, mdev 1.2, network 1.2, vendor modules 1.1). The factory firmware asks the coprocessor for the wake-up reason 0.44 s after power-on. (An earlier version of this entry put the network at about 15 s; that came from a first capture in which the two early-boot resets were still part of the interval.) The first boot after a software reboot reset itself again in the second capture, 3.2 s after "Starting mdev..." and about 7 s after the kernel started, both times; a hardware watchdog left armed by the previous run is a hypothesis only.

## The PIR, the sleep cycle, the boot time and the U-Boot changes (2026-11-23)

- The PIR sensor and the sleep cycle (the PIR belongs to the coprocessor; it is the motion source since stage 7, and the camera sleeps since stage 5). What follows is the record of 2026-11-23 in the order it was found, so its early sentences describe states that later ones changed. **Observed on 2026-11-23** (one unit, indoors, a hand waved at the lens, from a test build of the network driver run from RAM, see below): `8a:78 01 00` switched it on and every wave produced `0x03` alarm frames, `01` at the start and `00` at the end of a movement (five pairs for five waves); `8a:78 00 00` switched it off and nothing arrived in the next 50 s of waving. The coprocessor sent no answer to either command that the driver logs. A sensitivity command `8a:79` (level 1) was also sent; its effect is unknown. One alarm arrived before the switch-on command had been sent (after the sensitivity command), so the PIR may have been on from the start or switched on by that command; the factory log said `isEnablePir:0`. Detection distance, field of view, the time from movement to the frame, the cool-down between alarms and false alarms are not measured. The unit was left with the PIR off. **Standby (2026-11-23):** the factory sequence (`8a:77 07`, `9a:03`, `8a:61 01` on `0xa0`) switched the SoC off and the power button woke it (details in [factory firmware](factory-firmware.md)); the wake-ups by PIR, key and timer are untested because they need the changed `syslink_poll`, which is not installed. The sleep ran from a test driver loaded into RAM, so nothing sleeps by itself yet. **Update (2026-11-23, later):** stage 4 is installed on the camera (the two drivers replaced; nothing starts by itself). After `echo 780100` (PIR on) and the standby, the camera woke by itself when a hand was waved in front of the lens: the coprocessor reported `pir`, the new `syslink_poll` accepted it (`wakeup reason=pir`), and the camera was on the broker 11.6 s after the U-Boot banner. After the earlier wake by the power button (`poweron`) it took 33 s, because the Wi-Fi association had to be made again; why the PIR wake did not need that (a shorter sleep, about 50 s against about 2 min, or the reason) is not known. Wake-ups by the key and the timer are untested. **Observed on 2026-11-23 with the daemon's sleep running:** the camera was woken three times with the reason `timer`, each exactly 5 minutes after it went to sleep (13:11, 13:17 and 13:24 UTC), so the coprocessor has a default wake-up timer that we never switched off. With about 12 s of boot and 60 s of idle time that is more than a fifth of the time awake. Stage 6 sends the wake-up timer command `8a:7b` (0 = off, from the factory firmware: an assumption until it is seen to work) before every sleep. **Stage 5 and 6 (the sleep logic of the daemon, `sleep_after`, and the wake-up timer command), observed on 2026-11-23 on the camera on USB power with `sleep_after=60`, `sleep_usb=1` and `snapshot_delay_ms=1500`:** after a wake-up by the PIR the daemon reported motion `ON` with one snapshot 12 s after the U-Boot banner and the camera went to sleep again about 70 s later; the unattended cycle ran several times, each time with a clean disconnect (`availability` offline) before the power was cut. **The default 5-minute wake-ups by the coprocessor's timer stopped once the daemon sent `8a:7b` with 0 before the sleep:** the camera slept for more than 9 minutes without waking (before: every 5 minutes, three times in a row). **2026-11-23, after the video motion detector was switched off and the PIR became the only source:** the owner reported that the PIR woke the camera only when a hand was held about 5 cm from the lens; the corridor in front of the door is about 2 m wide, so a visitor is never farther away. The sensitivity of the PIR (the coprocessor setting `8a:79`; one value, level 1, was sent by mistake on 2026-11-23, so the factory value may have been changed) is therefore too low and is being tuned. **Boot time (measured on the unit, 2026-11-23):** U-Boot banner to Majestic 11.4 s at the start of the day, 8.9 s with stage 8, 7.9 s with `bootdelay` 0, **7.0 s with stage 9 and `quiet`**; to the broker 7.8 s. **From the U-Boot banner of a PIR wake-up to the person alert in Home Assistant: 13.9 s** (motion 9.0 s, picture 12.5 s, person 13.9 s), against about 20 s in the morning; **later the same day 11.8 s** with stage 10 and the ethernet driver left out (broker 5.6 s, motion 5.6 s, Majestic 6.05 s, picture 10.2 s, person 11.8 s; the kernel phase 3.0 s instead of 3.9 s), and the person check on the server answers a clear person in 0.2 s instead of about 1.1 s (two detectors, see the homelab). What is left is mostly fixed: U-Boot 1.6 s (USB scan and flash layout detection are in the bootloader), about 1 s between U-Boot and the kernel's first clock tick, about 0.9 s of OpenIPC's `/init` (flash checks and the JFFS2 overlay mount; it runs from the read-only root file system), the vendor modules about 1.2 s, and Majestic about 3 s from its start to the first picture at a cold start (1.33 s at a warm restart; resolution, slow shutter and frame rate change that by at most 0.12 s); the picture is now 1280 × 720 at quality 55 (about 77 KB, 1.9 s over the Wi-Fi) taken 700 ms after the motion, and Home Assistant waits for it in tenths of a second. The vendor video modules (1.7 s; the audio modules are only 0.2 s and are not needed) are now the longest step in Linux; the USB scan and the flash layout detection in U-Boot (about 0.6 s) are in the bootloader itself, not in its environment. **U-Boot `bootdelay` was set from 1 to 0 on 2026-11-23** with `fw_setenv bootdelay 0` after two identical reads of the 64 KB environment partition (`/dev/mtd1ro`); only that variable changed. It is no longer possible to stop the autoboot on the UART; recovery is the SPI programmer, the failsafe boot after two failed starts, and `fw_setenv bootdelay 1` from the running system. **Overlay clean-up (2026-11-23):** the old `*.previous`, `*.before-sleep` and `*.before-timer` backups were deleted (the binaries rebuild bit for bit from the repository, and the overlay was backed up twice before every stage) and `/root/.ash_history` (247 KB, rewritten at the end of every UART session, so flash wear) was replaced by a link to `/dev/null`. With the PIR at sensitivity level 3 (`8a:79`) a person about 1 m away was seen; at the lowest level only a hand 5 cm from the lens was. The wake-up by the person (a visitor in front of the peephole) and a day of battery use are not measured yet. A front-button press that wakes the camera is consumed by the coprocessor, so it does not start the LCD preview; the entities are unavailable while the camera sleeps. The test driver is `syslink_net_irq.ko` with the `mcu` parameter (source in the repository, binary not shipped).

## The default route (2026-11-20)

- A default route: stages 1 and 2 set only the on-link route, so the camera cannot reach another subnet (observed 2026-11-20: `nc` to a broker in another subnet failed with "Network unreachable" and succeeded after `ip route add default via <router>` in RAM). Stage 3 adds it from `gateway=` in `mqtt.conf`. The first on-camera boot of that path failed: BusyBox `ip route show default` prints the whole routing table, so the first version of `S97doorview-mqtt` concluded a default route existed. It was fixed (it now looks for a `default` line) and the tests' `ip` stand-in now behaves like BusyBox; the fixed hook then added `default via <router>` by itself on the next boot (observed 2026-11-20).

## Motion, night mode and person detection (2026-11-23)

- Motion detection: since 2026-11-23 the PIR of the coprocessor is the only source (stage 7, `pir_motion=1`; sensitivity 5, the factory app's highest setting, since stage 12); Majestic's video motion detector is off (`motionDetect` disabled in `/etc/majestic.yaml`, `motion_video=0`). **Observed on the unit:** a person about 1 m away wakes the camera and is reported as motion with a snapshot. The false-alarm rate (heat sources, sunlight on the door), missed visitors and night behaviour over days are not measured yet. Person detection runs on the server (below).

- Audio, the speaker and microphone, the battery fuel gauge beyond the MCU's value. The IR-cut filter and IR LEDs work through Majestic's `nightMode` with GPIO 8 (see [night vision](night-vision.md)); since 2026-11-23 Majestic goes to night from the sensor gain within about 3 s of its start (`autoNightGain: 16`, `autoNightDelay: 1`), observed after a restart in the dark; the first picture of a PIR wake-up waits for it since stage 13 (`night_wait_ms=4000`; **observed on 2026-11-23 after a real PIR wake-up in the dark:** the picture in Home Assistant was lit by the IR LEDs and the person clearly visible, still in colour with a violet cast because Majestic switches the IR-cut filter and the LEDs before the picture settings), and the day threshold (`autoDayGain: 4`) is a guess. In daylight the picture may wait up to about 1 s until Majestic has measured the light (not measured).

- Person detection. The factory firmware ran HiSilicon's `hi_ivp` detector on the camera after a PIR wake-up; OpenIPC has no such library. Under Linux the SoC has one core, about 27 MB of RAM and no neural accelerator, so classification runs elsewhere with the camera only triggering and sending a frame. **Observed (2026-11-23, one homelab):** a detector container (Faster R-CNN, COCO classes) beside Home Assistant classifies each archived snapshot in 1 to 2 s on the CPU and flags a person at 50 % confidence or more; on the 190 archived pictures it gave 99.6 % for the one picture of a person close to the lens after the camera was mounted, and below 40 % for an empty corridor at night. The sample is small (one real positive since mounting), so the recall for a person at the door, in the dark or far from the lens is not measured. Since 2026-11-23 the person alert is the only phone notification (there is none for motion), and a second, faster detector (SSD MobileNet) answers a clear person in about 0.2 s before the Faster R-CNN is asked (see the homelab).

## Waking from the network (2026-11-24)

All from a test build of the network driver loaded into RAM (`wifi` and `log_frames` parameters, branch `remote-wake-driver`), with the owner's approval for each new coprocessor command; nothing was written to the flash. The result is in [waking from the network](remote-wake.md).

- First, with the broker's address as the server (`10.0.70.202:1883`), an empty pattern and no keep-alive packet, sent while the SoC was on: the coprocessor connected at once from `10.0.50.190` (a source port of its own) and sent something Mosquitto rejected as a protocol error. The daemon's MQTT connection was then closed by the coprocessor's stack and Linux had no network until `9a:04` and a station reconnect.
- With an MQTT CONNECT for a user that does not exist as the keep-alive packet and the broker's refusal (`20 02 00 05`) as the pattern, then a standby: the coprocessor kept Wi-Fi while the SoC was off, connected about every 90 s, sent the packet 30 s after each connect (logged by Mosquitto as `dvwake ... not authorised`), and did not wake the SoC, neither on the refusal nor on the closed connection. The 5-minute RTC timer woke it with the reason `timer`; afterwards the coprocessor stopped connecting.
- A relay of our own (homelab, `doorview-wake` next to the broker, TCP 7967; the firewall and Cilium policy were changed for it): the coprocessor held the connection and sent the keep-alive every 30 s while the SoC slept. A 21-byte pattern, the pattern twice in one packet, an empty pattern with any data, the pattern 1.3 s after a keep-alive, with and without `9a:07`: no wake-up. With the SoC awake a pattern produced no message from the coprocessor.
- The coprocessor's own UART (1.8 V, read passively with the adapter's RX only) explained it: it reads the server's data 6 bytes at a time and compares each chunk with the pattern (`isWakeMatch = 0`), and it had stored the pattern length as 0 (`g_wakeupAttr.filterLen = 0`) for a pattern in the first entry of `9a:2c`. With every byte of `9a:2c` set to 6 it stored 6, and `06 06 06 06 06 06` from the relay woke the camera (`HI_EVENT_WIFI_WAKEUP`, `wakeupReason:4`, `send wakup reason:wifi`). That boot was reset by the coprocessor 10 s after the link came up (`syslink beatheart error, reboot camera now!`); the second boot (`poweron`) reached the broker 21.5 s after the pattern. A probe with byte *p* = *p*/5 + 1 gave `filterLen = 53`: the length is byte 260, the fourth entry's.
- A random 6-byte pattern in all four entries, as the factory firmware does: U-Boot about 2 s and the broker 7.9 s after the relay sent it, one clean boot. The camera went back to its normal sleep 60 s later and the coprocessor stopped the keep-alive.
- A series of six more network wake-ups (the same pattern, the camera re-armed from RAM each time): 8.8 s, 23.4 s, 23.3 s, 24.4 s, 22.8 s and 7.5 s from the pattern to the broker. The slow ones were two boots (two U-Boot banners on the console): the first printed its login prompt and was reset about 9 s later without reaching the broker; its kernel log could not be read before the reset. The clean boots reported `wakeup reason=wifi` in `dmesg`, the second boots `poweron`.
- Seen along the way: two `poweron` starts that nobody caused (one at 09:25 UTC while the camera was awake, one when its 600 s RTC timer was due); the coprocessor's RX log is full of `[hsl-error] chn tx sem post overflow` while the SoC sleeps; on a network wake-up it closes the PIR.
- The reset of the first boot after a network wake-up, found later the same day: a login into such a first boot (at 3.9 s of uptime, before the reset) showed `syslink_sdio: clock after=12000000` as the network driver's last line, with no `magic write`, no `RX GPIO` and `dv0` at 0 packets, while Majestic, the daemon and the console worked: the driver's SDIO setup had failed and its worker had ended without a message. Loading the driver again by hand at 4.2 s saved that boot (one U-Boot banner, broker 33 s after the pattern because of the station restart). Closing the relay's connection together with the pattern did not help (one of two still reset).
- Stage 14 (the driver trying its SDIO setup up to 15 times, the daemon arming the keep-alive before every standby) was installed on the unit at 14:16 UTC after two identical overlay reads. From the next start on, the camera re-armed itself every time: ten wake-ups from the relay in a row, 14:20 to 14:41 UTC, each one U-Boot banner on the console (ten in all) and the broker 8.4, 8.3, 8.0, 8.7, 8.3, 4.3, 7.9, 7.5, 8.0 and 8.5 s after the pattern; between them the daemon ran the live view for 30 s, slept 60 s later and armed the keep-alive again. The 4.3 s one is too short for a boot from the pattern (U-Boot alone comes about 2 s after it): the camera was probably already starting for another reason.
